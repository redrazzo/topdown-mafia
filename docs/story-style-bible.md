# Story And Style Bible

## Premise

The game is a grounded crime drama set in a fictional Northeast industrial port city between the late 1940s and early 1950s. The protagonist is a gifted wheelman trying to build a respectable life, but the city's power structure keeps offering "simple" jobs that carry him deeper into family business.

## Narrative pillars

- Loyalty creates debt faster than money can clear it.
- Respectability is a mask the city keeps tearing away.
- Violence is consequential, not theatrical.
- Status lives in cars, clothes, rooms, and who gets to make someone wait.
- The city should feel cold in public and warm in private.

## Visual direction

- Stylized-realistic, not photoreal.
- Wet streets, sodium warmth, deep navy nights, tobacco browns, brass accents.
- Strong silhouettes for cars, doors, stairwells, and interactables because the camera is high.
- Interiors should feel richer, warmer, and denser than the street.

## District identity

- Docks and warehouses for logistics jobs and dirty work.
- Brick and stone downtown blocks for business and family fronts.
- Immigrant residential streets with church pressure and neighborhood memory.
- Garages, diners, clubs, stairwells, and back offices as recurring story spaces.

## Slice cast

- Protagonist: the wheelman on the edge of compromise.
- Vincent: the contact who frames the first "favor" as routine.
- Off-screen boss: only hinted at in slice one, felt more than seen.

## Chapter one dramatic shape

1. Report to a curbside meeting.
2. Take possession of a car and package.
3. Drive through a small district with rising tension.
4. Reach a back-office destination.
5. Hand off the package and realize this was not harmless work.
6. Leave with the sense that return is no longer possible.
